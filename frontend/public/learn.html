Why estimate a loss before it happens?

A lender does not wait until every borrower misses every payment before thinking about loss. IFRS 9 asks lenders to make a sensible, documented estimate of the credit loss that may happen in the future. That estimate is called Expected Credit Loss, or ECL.

1

Look at each loan

RiskFrame reads the loan's probability of default, loss severity, exposure and stage.

2

Estimate its loss

It multiplies those three simple ingredients for every loan, using a horizon that matches its stage.

3

Add up the portfolio

The loan estimates are summed into a portfolio provision. You can inspect the inputs behind the total.

The core calculation

ECL is three plain ideas multiplied together.

The engine does not use a black box. For each loan it uses its chance of default, the share expected to be lost if default happens, and the amount exposed at that time.

Expected Credit Loss for one loan
ECL = PD × LGD × EAD

Probability of Default × Loss Given Default × Exposure at Default

PD used8%
LGD used55%
EAD used$10,000
Estimated ECL$440

Worked example: 0.08 × 0.55 × $10,000 = $440. RiskFrame repeats this calculation loan by loan, then adds the results.

First ingredient

PD asks: how likely is default?

Start with the 12-month PD

A point-in-time 12-month PD is the starting estimate of a borrower defaulting in the next year. A value of 0.08 means an 8% probability, not a promise that eight in every hundred borrowers will default.

RiskFrame keeps this original input and calculates a calibrated version for ECL.

Calibrate it to what was observed

When calibration is switched on, the engine checks whether the portfolio's average starting PD is aligned with its observed default rate.

calibration multiplier = observed default rate ÷ average raw PD

The calibrated PD is the raw PD multiplied by this factor and then kept safely between 0.0001% and 99.99%.

Why calibrate? A score can be directionally useful but too optimistic or pessimistic for the actual portfolio. Calibration makes its average level more consistent with observed defaults. It is still an estimate, not a forecast guarantee.
Choosing the horizon

The stage decides how far ahead RiskFrame looks.

IFRS 9 uses stages to distinguish loans with ordinary credit risk from loans where credit risk has increased substantially or default has occurred. In RiskFrame, the file stage is used in the ECL calculation. When days past due are available, a simple rule stage is also shown so you can compare the two.

Stage 1

12-month expected loss

The borrower is not treated as having a significant increase in credit risk. ECL uses the calibrated 12-month PD.

PD used = PD calibrated

Stage 2

Lifetime expected loss

Credit risk is considered to have increased. ECL uses a cumulative PD over the configured lifetime, five years by default.

PD life = 1 − (1 − PD)ⁿ

Stage 3

Credit-impaired

The borrower is considered credit-impaired. The engine uses PD of 1.0, so the remaining loss estimate is driven by LGD and EAD.

PD used = 1.0

From one year to a lifetime

A 10% annual PD over five years is not simply 50%. RiskFrame uses survival maths: it calculates the chance of avoiding default every year and subtracts it from one.

1 − (1 − 0.10)⁵ = 40.95%

The transparent DPD rule

When days past due is present, the rule stage is: Stage 3 for a default flag or 90+ DPD; Stage 2 for 30 to 89 DPD; otherwise Stage 1. The dashboard shows its agreement with the file stage.

The remaining ingredients

LGD estimates what is not recovered. EAD estimates what is at risk.

Loss Given Default, or LGD

LGD is the portion of exposure expected not to be recovered if a borrower defaults. An LGD of 0.55 means an expected loss of 55 cents for every dollar exposed after recoveries, collateral and other protections are considered.

When an LGD data file is supplied, RiskFrame uses its EAD-weighted LGD: sum(EAD × LGD) ÷ sum(EAD). Bigger exposures therefore have an appropriate influence on the headline.

Exposure at Default, or EAD

EAD is the amount expected to be outstanding at the time of default. RiskFrame can take it from an ead column, a balance column, or a configured global default when a source column is not selected.

It never uses a negative exposure. If EAD or LGD is a proxy rather than an observed value, the dashboard labels the calculation as an estimate rather than a booked figure.

Average 12-month PD is not the ECL PD

The dashboard labels the average 12-month PD clearly. ECL itself uses a stage-weighted PD: 12-month PD in Stage 1, lifetime PD in Stage 2, and 1.0 in Stage 3. That is why the provision cannot be recreated from one average PD alone.

Decision tools

Scenario stress testing

Baseline, Adverse and Upside scenarios adjust PD and LGD by simple multipliers. Their ECL values are combined using normalised weights, so the weights always total 100%. In the bundled sample, weighted ECL is $17.85m, a 13.2% uplift

Single-factor sensitivity

RiskFrame changes PD, LGD or EAD by one shock at a time, holding the other two unchanged. The tornado chart shows the ECL range from the low to high shock. It intentionally does not simulate macroeconomic factors because the PD is already calibrated, and a macro overlay here would be invented rather than evidenced.

Borrower what-if

You can change one borrower's DPD, internal score or starting PD in a temporary calculation. Nothing is saved. If DPD moves from 15 to 45, it crosses the 30-day line, changes the rule stage to Stage 2 and switches the ECL horizon from 12-month to lifetime PD.

Early-warning watchlist

When DPD exists, the watchlist finds rule Stage 1 accounts at 25 to 29 DPD, just below the 30-day line. It compares their current 12-month ECL with the lifetime ECL they would have in Stage 2 and ranks the difference. It is a watchlist, not a prediction that a stage move will happen.

Trust the inputs

Data health warns, but does not quietly rewrite the story.

Checks the app runs

  • Missing required portfolio fields
  • PD values outside 0 to 1
  • Invalid file stages
  • Non-positive EAD where an EAD column is used
  • Negative days past due
  • Duplicate account IDs

What the health chip means

Green means no check found an issue. Amber means the data has warnings to inspect. Red means at least one error-level issue exists. The health indicator warns you and shows sample IDs, but it does not silently block your analysis or alter your file.

Your request, only for this calculation

Nothing is stored on the server.

RiskFrame is stateless. The bundled sample is read fresh when you select it. For an uploaded CSV, the browser parses it and sends rows only with the analysis request. The server creates in-memory data frames, calculates the response, then retains no portfolio, LGD file, configuration or borrower what-if data for the next request.
An honest boundary

Useful estimates still have limits.

This is not a booked accounting figure

Results depend on the supplied PD, LGD, EAD, stage and configuration. A default LGD or EAD is a practical proxy, not a substitute for a governed finance process. RiskFrame does not account for discounting, forward-looking macroeconomic modelling, model governance or audit sign-off.

Cross-sectional data has a limit too

The bundled portfolio has one row per account. Its monthly chart is an aggregate ECL and stage-mix trend across report dates. It does not claim to show account-level stage migration or month-on-month bridges, because the data cannot support that conclusion.

Quick reference

Glossary

PD
Probability of Default: the estimated chance that a borrower defaults over a stated time horizon.
LGD
Loss Given Default: the share of exposure expected not to be recovered after default.
EAD
Exposure at Default: the amount expected to be outstanding when a default happens.
ECL
Expected Credit Loss: PD × LGD × EAD, calculated for each loan and summed for the portfolio.
Lifetime PD
The cumulative probability of default over the configured number of years.
DPD
Days Past Due: the number of days a payment is overdue, used in RiskFrame's transparent stage rule when available.
Provision
The portfolio ECL amount, often shown as a percentage of total EAD.
